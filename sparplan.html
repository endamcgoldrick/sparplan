    Glossary

    What the numbers mean

    Risk level (1–7)
    The EU's standard scale (ESMA SRRI). It's set by how much the price swung each year over 5 years: 1 under 0.5%, 2 under 2%, 3 under 5%, 4 under 10%, 5 under 15%, 6 under 25%, 7 at 25% or more.
    Swings per year (volatility)
    How far the price typically moves up or down in a year. 15% means a normal year can easily land 15% above or below average.
    Biggest drop
    The largest fall from a high point to a later low. It's the loss you'd have seen if you bought at the worst moment.
    Reward for risk (Sharpe ratio)
    Return above what risk-free Swedish T-bills paid (avg –), divided by the swings. Above 0.5 is good, below 0 means T-bills did better.
    Time horizon
    Follows from the risk level. Levels 1–2 are short (under 2 years), 3–4 medium (2–5 years), 5–7 long (5 years or more), the time usually needed to recover from a bad drop.
    Currency effect
    Holdings traded in EUR or USD are measured in SEK, so currency moves are part of their swings. The funds trade in EUR because EU rules generally let Swedish savers buy only EU-registered (UCITS) funds. Each one is labelled with where its money is invested, such as Global, US or Japan, not where it trades. That's why euro bond funds land at level 4 here.
    Method: weekly dividend-adjusted closes for 5 years from Yahoo Finance, converted to SEK at the Riksbank's official EUR/SEK fixing. Volatility is the standard deviation of weekly log returns × √52. Sharpe uses the 5-year average 3-month T-bill rate. Past results are not forecasts. The official SRI on a fund's KID also adds credit risk and may differ. This page is for learning, not a recommendation to buy anything. Places to buy these in Sweden: Avanza, Nordnet, or your bank.
